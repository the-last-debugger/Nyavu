# The band words: what else could they be

The current three are **clear / caution / concern**. The alliteration was accidental, and so was a
fault: they are not the same kind of word.

| word | part of speech | what it describes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| clear | adjective | the address |
| caution | noun | advice to the reader |
| concern | noun | a feeling in the reader |

A scale has to be one thing measured three times. Right now one rung describes the address and the
other two describe people, which is why the set feels less solid than it looks.

## What the guidance says

GOV.UK's [clear language](https://guidance.publishing.service.gov.uk/writing-to-gov-uk-standards/writing-guidelines/clear-language/)
standard asks for plain English, active voice, short sentences, and warns against jargon and
buzzwords. Its test is simple: would a person use this word themselves? Applied literally:

- **clear** passes. People say a link looks clear.
- **caution** is a sign on a road, not a description of an address.
- **concern** is what a doctor records, not what a name is.

The same standard says name the thing rather than the technique, which is why the intent line says
"get a password" and not "credential harvesting".

## Options

Each set is all adjectives, all describing the address, and rises in severity.

| set | words | feel | risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **A** | clean / suspect / dangerous | plain, spoken, honest at the middle | "suspect" is also a noun; in a pill it reads fine |
| **B** | clear / doubtful / dangerous | calmest, closest to today | "doubtful" can mean "unlikely", a small ambiguity |
| **C** | sound / strained / unsafe | engineering register, matches "weighing" | "strained" is a metaphor that needs a beat to land |
| **D** | ordinary / odd / alarming | matches the existing "This reads as ordinary" | "odd" is understated for a phishing verdict |
| **E** | clear / caution / concern | today | mixed parts of speech |

## Recommendation: A, clean / suspect / dangerous

- one part of speech, so the scale reads as one scale;
- plain words a reader already owns;
- **suspect** is the honest middle: suspicion is exactly what a name can support, and it does not
  claim proof;
- the severity ordering is obvious without a legend.

It also survives the three places the words appear: the dial readout (`suspect`), the sentence
("This address reads suspect"), and the fold ("Bands: under 35 clean, 35 to 65 suspect, above 65
dangerous").

If you want the calmest option, B keeps `clear` and only changes the two that do not fit.

## Not recommended

- **scam / fraud** at the top: a legal claim, not a reading of a name.
- **threat / malicious**: security-vendor language, and we only saw the name.
- **low / medium / high**: true, but it is a severity dial, not a sentence, and it was already
  rejected in favour of words.
