Check a link

Checklist

Is this link safe?

You do not need to be technical to judge a link. You need to look at the right part of it, and to know which signals are worthless.

Step 1, find the part that counts

Read the address from the right, not the left. The last label before the ending is the name somebody registered.

Take https://safaricom.co.ke.secure-login.top/verify. The eye stops at safaricom.co.ke and relaxes. But the address is read from the right: top is the ending, secure-login is the name, so the real domain is secure-login.top. Everything to its left is a label the owner can invent for free, including a bank's name.

Step 2, compare it to what you expected

Say the address aloud, character by character, against the domain you know. Most phishing addresses differ by one character: rn for m, l for I, a doubled letter, a hyphen inserted where there should be none.

Step 3, ask what the message wants

Phishing needs you to act before you think. A link that arrives with a deadline, an account closing, a payment reversing, a parcel returning, is using urgency as a tool. The urgency is the tell, not the fact.

Step 4, reach the organisation another way

This is the step that makes the first three almost unnecessary. If a message claims to be from your bank, open your banking app, or type the bank's address yourself, or call the number on your card. You do not need to judge the link if you never use it.

Signals that mean nothing

  • The padlock. HTTPS proves the connection is encrypted, not that the site is honest. Certificates are free and most phishing sites have one.
  • A familiar sender. Mailboxes get compromised. A friend's address sending you a link is weak evidence, not strong evidence.
  • Good design. A page can be copied perfectly in seconds.
  • It worked before. Attackers reuse domains until they are blocked.

When you have already clicked

  1. Do not enter anything else on the page.
  2. If you typed a password, change it from the organisation's app or website, and anywhere you reused it.
  3. If you entered card details, call your bank and ask them to watch or replace the card.
  4. Report the message, to your provider, and to the organisation being imitated.
