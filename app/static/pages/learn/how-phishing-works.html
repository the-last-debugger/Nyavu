How phishing links work, and how to spot one

A phishing link rarely looks obviously wrong. It usually looks almost right, which is exactly why it works. These are the five tricks the addresses depend on.

1. A spelling that survives a glance

Swap one letter and most people read the word they expect. safaricom.co.ke becomes safaricorn.co.ke, an m replaced by rn. The eye resolves the shape, the hand clicks, and the page is an exact copy of the real login screen.

2. The brand buried in a subdomain

Read this carefully: safaricom.co.ke.verify-account.top. It contains the bank's name, so it looks legitimate at a glance. But a domain is read from the right, not the left. Everything before verify-account.top is invented, the attacker owns only the end.

This is the single most common trick, and it defeats people who have been told to "check the domain" without being told which part of it counts.

3. Characters that look identical

Some alphabets contain letters that render almost exactly like Latin ones. A Cyrillic а is not an a. To a reader they are the same glyph; to a browser they are different addresses. This is a homoglyph attack, and no amount of careful reading catches it , only inspecting the underlying address does.

4. A cheap ending

Registration in some top-level domains costs almost nothing and requires no identity checks. When a bank's name appears on a .tk, .top or .xyz address, that combination is worth treating as suspicious on its own, the organisation almost certainly owns a country-code or .com domain instead.

5. No name at all, just a number

http://197.248.11.9/equity/login.php is a real pattern. There is no domain to check because there is no domain, just a server address. Legitimate organisations publish names, not numbers.

What does not prove a link is safe

  • A padlock. HTTPS encrypts the connection; it says nothing about who is on the other end. Most phishing sites now have valid certificates.
  • It arrived from someone you know. Accounts get compromised; a familiar sender is weak evidence.
  • It looks professional. Page copies are downloaded whole, logos included.

How to check one

  1. Find the registered domain, the last two labels, or the last three for things like .co.ke. That is the part someone had to buy.
  2. Compare that domain to the one you expect, character by character, not by shape.
  3. If the message is urgent, slow down deliberately. Urgency is a technique, not a fact.
  4. Reach the organisation the way you normally would, their app, your bookmark, the number on your card, rather than through the link you were sent.
