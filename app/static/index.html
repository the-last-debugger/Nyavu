How it reads an address

01

It cuts to the registered name

Only the registered domain is judged, the part somebody actually buys and owns. Everything before it is free to invent, so login.bank.co.ke.evil.top is read as evil.top.

02

It measures seventeen things

Length, digits, hyphens, character entropy, the top-level domain, whether a brand name appears in the wrong place, and how close the spelling sits to a known brand.

03

It says why, not just what

A logistic regression turns those measurements into a risk score, and the same coefficients produce the reasons you read. Nothing is fetched from the destination.

What it cannot do

Nyavu judges names, not pages. A phishing page on a compromised legitimate site passes, because its address really is correct; and a domain inside the Tranco top 100,000 is never flagged on name evidence alone. Accuracy on our held-out corpus is 75.4%, a verdict is an assessment, not a guarantee, and a second opinion is always worth the minute.

About the model

Three classifiers were trained on the same features and compared on a domain-disjoint hold-out set.

What the score means

The score is the model's estimated probability that an address is a phish, multiplied by 100. It is a probability, not a count of faults: an established domain usually lands somewhere between 1 and 20, because a classifier's estimate is rarely exactly zero, and an address with three clear warning signs usually lands above 80. The bands are drawn at 35 and 65, which is where the classifier's own error rates made the trade worth making.
